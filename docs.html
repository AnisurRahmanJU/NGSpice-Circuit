Simple Circuit Examples

Definition

Topic 2 · 10 examples

DC Resistor Circuits

Definition

Networks of resistors only. Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's laws decide every voltage and current.

How to create the circuit

Place resistors in series, parallel or bridge form, add a DC source, wire them, run .dc and plot node voltages.

Why it is important

Almost every analog circuit contains a resistor network, so this is the base of circuit analysis.

Open full simulator

Related applications in real life

  • Volume and brightness controls
  • Battery level sensing
  • Weighing scales (Wheatstone bridge)
  • Keypad resistor ladders
Topic 3 · 12 examples

Transient: RC, RL, RLC

Definition

Transient analysis shows how voltage and current change with time after a switch or pulse in RC, RL and RLC circuits.

How to create the circuit

Add a pulse or step source, a resistor with a capacitor or inductor, wire them, set .tran with a stop time, then plot over time.

Why it is important

It explains charging, delay, ringing and time constants, which set the speed of every real circuit.

Open full simulator

Related applications in real life

  • Camera flash charging
  • Button debounce in remote controls
  • Soft start in power supplies
  • Delay for wiper and indicator timing

AC Frequency Response and Filters

Definition

Frequency response shows how a circuit passes or blocks different frequencies. Filters keep wanted frequencies only.

How to create the circuit

Build an RC, RL or RLC network, add an AC source, set .ac with a start and stop frequency, then plot gain against frequency.

Why it is important

Filters clean signals and separate bands, so audio, radio and measurement systems all depend on them.

Related applications in real life

  • Radio station tuning
  • Noise filters in phone chargers
  • Speaker crossovers (woofer and tweeter)

Diodes and Rectifiers

Definition

A diode lets current flow in one direction only. Rectifiers use this to turn AC into DC.

How to create the circuit

Connect an AC source, diode(s) and a load, add a smoothing capacitor if needed, run .tran and compare input with output.

Why it is important

Rectification is the first step of nearly every power supply and signal detector.

Related applications in real life

  • Phone and laptop chargers
  • AM radio detection
  • Car alternator rectifiers
  • Reverse-polarity protection
Topic 6 · 14 examples

BJT Circuits

Definition

A BJT is a current-controlled transistor. A small base current controls a larger collector current.

How to create the circuit

Place an NPN or PNP transistor, add bias resistors and a supply, run .op or .dc, then plot the collector voltage.

Why it is important

It gives amplification and switching, the core job of analog and digital electronics.

Open full simulator

Related applications in real life

  • Audio amplifiers
  • Driving LEDs and relays from sensors
  • Doorbell and buzzer drivers
  • Radio transmitters
Topic 7 · 12 examples

MOSFET Circuits

Definition

A MOSFET is a voltage-controlled transistor. The gate voltage sets the drain current with almost no gate current.

How to create the circuit

Place an N or P MOSFET, set gate bias and a drain resistor, run .dc to plot the characteristic, or .tran to see switching.

Why it is important

MOSFETs are the building block of modern chips and efficient power switching.

Open full simulator

Related applications in real life

  • Computer processors and memory
  • Electric vehicle motor drives
  • Laptop power regulators
  • LED dimmers

Definition

Controlled sources and op-amps produce an output that depends on another voltage or current in the circuit.

555 Timer Circuits

Definition

The 555 is a timer chip that works as an astable oscillator, a monostable pulse or a modulator, set by an RC network.

How to create the circuit

Place the 555 subcircuit, connect R1, R2 and C for timing, add a 5 V to 15 V supply, run .tran and plot the output.

Why it is important

One cheap chip makes accurate timing, which replaces many parts in simple designs.

Open full simulator

Related applications in real life

  • Turn-signal flashers
  • Smoke-alarm style beepers
  • LED dimmers with PWM
  • Toy sound generators

Relay Circuits (10 real-life examples)

Definition

A relay is an electromagnetic switch. A small coil current closes contacts that carry a bigger load.

How to create the circuit

Place the relay, wire its coil to a control source and switch, wire the contacts to the load supply, then run and click the switch.

Why it is important

Relays isolate a weak control circuit from a strong or high-voltage load.

Related applications in real life

  • Home automation switching
  • Washing machine controllers
  • Industrial motor starters

It steps voltage up or down and gives electrical isolation for safety.
